The financial infrastructure revolution that's already happening behind the scenes

The AI Agent Spending Explosion

We're witnessing an unprecedented shift in how businesses operate. AI agents aren't just fancy chatbots anymore; they're autonomous economic actors making real purchasing decisions at scale:

  • Cloud Compute: Agents automatically scaling GPU clusters based on workload demands, sometimes spending thousands per hour
  • API Credits: Purchasing additional calls to GPT-4, Claude, or specialized AI services when quota runs low
  • SaaS Tools: Subscribing to Zapier, Notion, Airtable, and dozens of other tools to complete their workflows
  • Data Sources: Buying access to real-time market data, news feeds, or specialized databases
  • Digital Services: Everything from domain registrations to email automation platforms

According to our research, companies running production AI agents see an average of 47 automated purchases per month. That number is growing 23% quarter-over-quarter as agents become more sophisticated and autonomous.

Why Corporate Cards Aren't Built for This

Traditional expense management solutions like Ramp, Brex, and Expensify were designed for humans making predictable purchases. They assume someone will take a photo of a receipt, categorize the expense, and maybe add a memo explaining the business purpose.

But AI agents don't have thumbs. They can't snap receipt photos or write expense reports. They make purchases programmatically, often in real-time, based on complex decision trees that would take paragraphs to explain.

Here's what breaks when you give agents access to your corporate card:

  • No Real-Time Policy Enforcement: Can't stop an agent from overspending until it's too late
  • Manual Reconciliation Nightmare: Someone has to manually categorize hundreds of AI-generated transactions
  • Security Risks: Full card details exposed to AI systems that might log or inadvertently share them
  • Budget Black Holes: No way to set agent-specific spending limits or track ROI by agent
  • Compliance Headaches: Auditors have no idea why an AI bought a $3,000 database subscription at 2 AM
